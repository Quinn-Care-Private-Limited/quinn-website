How to add an Instagram feed to your website

Putting your Instagram feed on your site is one of the cheapest trust signals available: it shows the brand is alive, shows real people using the product, and gives visitors something to look at that is not a product grid. There are four ways to do it, they differ mainly in maintenance and speed cost, and only one of them turns the feed into something that sells.

The four options

1. Instagram's official embed

Instagram gives you an embed code per post. Copy it, paste it into a page, done.

Good: free, official, no third party. Bad: it embeds one post at a time, not a feed — so a wall of nine posts means nine embeds you update by hand forever. It also loads Instagram's script, which is heavy.

2. A feed widget or app

A tool connects to your account and renders your latest posts automatically, so the feed stays current without you touching it.

Good: automatic, styleable, usually installable without code. Bad: another subscription, and quality varies wildly on page-speed impact — some widgets are notorious for it.

3. Custom code against the Instagram API

A developer builds it directly against Meta's Basic Display or Graph API.

Good: total control, no vendor. Bad: access tokens expire and need refreshing, Meta changes the API periodically, and when it breaks it breaks silently. Only worth it if you have engineers and a strong reason.

4. A shoppable feed app

Same as a widget, except the products in each post or reel are tagged, so a visitor who likes what they see can buy without going hunting.

Good: the feed stops being decoration and becomes a sales surface, with attribution telling you which posts earned money. Bad: overkill if you are not selling anything.

The mistake most stores make

They install the feed, it looks good, and it does nothing measurable. That is because a normal embedded feed is a dead end: a visitor sees a reel of a product they want, and the only available action is leaving your site for Instagram. You have taken traffic you already own and pointed it at someone else's app.

Making the feed shoppable inverts that. The same reel becomes a tappable product card with add-to-cart inside it, and because every tap and order is attributed per post, you can finally answer "does our Instagram content actually sell anything?" with a number instead of a feeling.

Keeping it fast

Instagram feeds are a common cause of slow pages, because they load images, video and third-party scripts. Four rules: lazy-load anything below the fold, cap the number of posts (nine to twelve is plenty), avoid widgets that inject large scripts, and make sure video is compressed rather than served at source size. Questions

How do I embed my Instagram feed on my website?

You have four options: Instagram's official per-post embed code (free but manual, one post at a time), a feed widget or app that syncs automatically, a custom build against Meta's API, or a shoppable feed app that also tags products so visitors can buy. For most businesses a widget or app is the right trade-off.

Can I embed an Instagram feed for free?

Yes — Instagram's official embed code is free, and many feed widgets have free tiers. The catch with the official embed is that it handles one post at a time and must be updated manually, so it does not stay current on its own.

Does an Instagram feed slow down a website?

It can, because feeds load images, video and third-party scripts. Lazy-load below-the-fold content, cap the feed at nine to twelve posts, avoid script-heavy widgets, and make sure video is compressed rather than served at full size.

How do I make my Instagram feed shoppable?

Use an app that lets you tag products to each post or reel so visitors can add to cart without leaving. On Shopify, Quinn imports reels by link, tags products to them, and reports add-to-carts and orders per post so you can see which content actually sells.

Why is my embedded Instagram feed not showing posts?

Usually an expired access token or a permissions change on the connected account — the most common failure mode of custom API builds. Managed widgets and apps handle token refresh for you, which is the main reason not to hand-roll it.
