Platform comparison

Shoppable video platforms, compared

Every shoppable video platform does the same core job: put video on your store and let shoppers buy from inside it. What actually separates them is how they charge — flat plans versus metering by views, clicks, impressions or AI tokens — and how much platform you have to adopt to get there. Here is the honest breakdown, including where Quinn is the wrong choice.

Free plan · $9–$49/mo flat · Unlimited at $49 · ~5 min setup

The short version

If you run a Shopify store and want videos and reels to be shoppable, almost any tool on this list will work. The decision comes down to three things: what you pay as you grow, whether you want a widget or a platform, and how much your theme has to change. Most of the cost surprises in this category come from metering — you buy a plan for one number of views and get repriced when a video does well.

The platforms

Videowise

The most complete video-commerce platform in the category: shoppable video, live commerce, hosting, and deep analytics, aimed at larger brands with a video program to run. Pricing is modular and enterprise-shaped — expect hundreds per month once you are past the entry tier. Choose it if you have a dedicated video team and want one system for everything. Tolstoy

A broad AI video-commerce suite — shoppable video plus interactive/branching video, quizzes and AI features. Pricing is metered by impressions and AI tokens, with published tiers running to roughly $499/mo before enterprise. Choose it if you want interactive branching video and AI merchandising, not just a video widget. Quinn vs Tolstoy →

ReelUp

A well-liked Shopify-native shoppable reels app with speed-optimised delivery. The free plan is limited (around 100 views/month) and the entry paid tier is about $29.99/mo for roughly 3,000 views. Choose it if its specific widget set matches your theme. Quinn vs ReelUp →

Vidjet

Clean, well-designed video widgets with a strong template library. There is no free tier, and pricing is view-metered from about $49/mo for 4,000 views up toward $499/mo. Choose it if you want their formats and your view volume is predictable. Quinn vs Vidjet →

Whatmore

Shoppable reels with a click-metered model — a free tier at roughly 300 clicks, then about $29/mo, $79/mo at around 15,000 clicks, and up. Choose it if click metering happens to suit your traffic shape. Quinn vs Whatmore →

Quinn

A Shopify app built to be the simple, flat-priced option: import Instagram reels and TikToks by link, make them shoppable in carousels, stories, floating widgets and grids, and pay a flat $9–$49/mo with no metering surprises. The $49 Unlimited plan is genuinely unlimited on views, videos and pages. Choose it if you want the widget without adopting a platform. Do not choose it if you need live streaming, branching/interactive video, or a full video CMS — those are real gaps, and Videowise or Tolstoy serve them better.

Two things worth checking that buyers usually miss

Built for Shopify

Built for Shopify is a badge Shopify awards, not a claim an app makes about itself. To earn it an app has to meet Shopify's bar on performance, use of the modern extensibility model (no theme-code injection), merchant reviews and support responsiveness — and it can be revoked. Of the apps on this list, only Quinn and ReelUp carry it. It is the single easiest credential to verify yourself: open any app's Shopify App Store listing and look for the badge.

What the app does to your page speed

This is the question merchants ask us most, and the one the category answers least specifically. Video is the heaviest asset you can add to a storefront and every widget ships JavaScript, so a shoppable-video app can quietly cost more in conversion than it earns. Almost every vendor says "optimised" and "lightning fast"; very few publish what they actually do.

How to actually choose

  • Pick by your growth curve, not your first month. A metered plan that is cheap at 3,000 views gets expensive exactly when a video works. Model the cost at 10× your current traffic before you commit.
  • Decide widget or platform. If you only need videos to be shoppable, a widget is faster to install and cheaper to run. If you are running live events and interactive video, buy the platform.
  • Check site speed. Video is heavy. Ask how each tool compresses and serves video, and whether it lazy-loads. Quinn compresses up to 20× and serves from a CDN, so pages stay fast.
  • Check attribution. "Views" are not revenue. You want add-to-cart and order attribution at the page and media level, so you know which video earned the money.
  • Check the install. Anything that needs theme-code edits is a change you will have to redo at the next theme update.

Questions

What is the best shoppable video platform?

It depends on whether you want a widget or a platform. For a Shopify store that wants videos and reels shoppable at a predictable price, Quinn is the flat-priced option ($9–$49/mo, unlimited at $49) and one of only two apps here carrying the Built for Shopify badge. It is also the only one publishing its performance engineering: a VMAF 93 per-scene encoding target, faststart single-stream delivery with no manifest round-trip, and sub-10 KB per-component widget bundles. For live commerce and a full video programme, Videowise. For interactive and branching video, Tolstoy.

Which shoppable video app is best for site speed?

Judge it on four published specifics rather than marketing adjectives: the encoding quality target (Quinn targets VMAF 93), whether encoding is per-scene or per-file (Quinn is per-scene, so each scene hits its own minimum size), how playback starts (Quinn uses faststart and a single stream, avoiding the two manifest fetches adaptive-bitrate players need first), and widget bundle size (Quinn ships under 10 KB per component, loading only what your design uses). Most vendors publish none of these — ask, and verify in DevTools.

How much do shoppable video platforms cost?

Entry tiers run from free to about $49/mo, but the real number depends on metering. Most platforms charge by views, clicks, impressions or AI tokens, so the cost rises as your videos perform. Quinn uses flat plans — $9, $19, or $49/mo — and the $49 Unlimited plan does not meter views, videos or pages.

Do shoppable videos slow down a website?

They can, because video is heavy. The fix is compression, CDN delivery and lazy loading. Quinn compresses video up to 20× with AI and serves it lazily from a CDN so the effect on page speed is close to zero. Ask any vendor how they handle this before installing.

Which shoppable video platform has a free plan?

Quinn, Tolstoy, ReelUp and Whatmore all have free tiers, though the allowances differ a lot — Quinn's free plan covers about 550 views a month, ReelUp's around 100, and Whatmore's about 300 clicks. Vidjet has no free tier and starts around $49/mo.

Can I use Instagram reels and TikToks as shoppable videos?

Yes. Most tools let you import social video, and Quinn imports Instagram reels and TikToks by link, then makes them shoppable on your store. You keep the content you already made instead of shooting anything new.
