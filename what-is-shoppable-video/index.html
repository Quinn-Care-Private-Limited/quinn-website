What is shoppable video?

Shoppable video is video with the checkout built in: a shopper watches, taps the product they can see on screen, and adds it to cart or buys without ever leaving the video. It turns content that used to just build awareness into something that directly produces orders — and it is the closest a website gets to the way people already shop on TikTok and Instagram.

The definition

Shoppable video is any video where the products shown are tappable and purchasable inside the player. Instead of watching a clip and then hunting for the product, the viewer taps a tagged item and gets a product card, an add-to-cart button, or a direct checkout. The video becomes a storefront rather than an ad for one.

You will also see it called video commerce, interactive video, or shoppable UGC. The mechanics are the same: video plus product tagging plus an in-player buying path.

How it works, step by step

  1. You bring video. A product clip, a customer's unboxing, an Instagram reel or a TikTok you already posted.
  2. You tag products to it. Each video is linked to one or more products from your catalogue — and for multi-product videos, tags can be timed to the moments each item appears.
  3. It gets embedded as a widget. A carousel on the homepage, a stories bar, a floating bubble that follows the shopper, a grid on a collection page, or a block on the product page.
  4. The shopper taps and buys. The product card opens over the video; add-to-cart or checkout happens inline, so nobody loses their place.
  5. You attribute the revenue. Good tools track views, taps, add-to-carts and orders per video, so you can tell which content actually sold.

Why it works

Three reasons, in order of importance:

  • It answers the questions product photos cannot. How a fabric moves, how big something really is, how a product is used. That is the hesitation that kills conversion, and video removes it.
  • It removes steps. Every extra click between wanting something and buying it costs orders. Tagging the product inside the video collapses that path to one tap.
  • It matches the habit people already have. Shoppers are trained by TikTok and Reels to browse by video. A store that behaves the same way feels familiar rather than effortful.

What it costs

Shoppable video is one of the cheaper conversion tools to add, because you usually already own the content. Free tiers exist across the category; paid plans commonly start around $9–$49/mo. The thing to watch is metering — many platforms charge by views, clicks or impressions, so the bill grows precisely when a video performs. Flat pricing avoids that: Quinn's plans are $9, $19 and $49/mo, and the $49 Unlimited plan does not meter views, videos or pages.

What to watch out for

  • Site speed. Video is the heaviest thing you can put on a page. Insist on compression, CDN delivery and lazy loading, or your Core Web Vitals will pay for it.
  • Theme-code edits. Anything requiring manual theme changes has to be redone when you change or update your theme.
  • Vanity metrics. Views are not revenue. Insist on add-to-cart and order attribution per video.
  • Content you do not have. If you have no video at all, start with what exists — your Instagram reels, TikToks, and customer clips — before commissioning a shoot.

Questions

What is shoppable video?

Shoppable video is video where the products on screen are tappable and purchasable inside the player. A shopper watches, taps a product, and adds to cart or checks out without leaving the video, which turns content into a direct sales channel rather than just awareness.

What is the difference between shoppable video and normal product video?

A normal product video shows the product and hopes the viewer goes and finds it. A shoppable video has the product tagged inside it, so the viewer can buy in one tap. The content can be identical — the difference is the buying path and the ability to attribute revenue per video.

Does shoppable video actually increase conversion?

It helps where hesitation is the problem — fit, size, texture, how something is used — because video answers questions photos cannot, and tagging removes clicks between wanting and buying. Results vary by store and content, so use a tool that attributes add-to-carts and orders per video and measure it on your own traffic.
