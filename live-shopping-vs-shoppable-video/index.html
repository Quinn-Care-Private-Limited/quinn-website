Live shopping vs shoppable video

Live shopping is a scheduled live stream where viewers buy in real time. Shoppable video is recorded video on your store with products tagged inside it. They get lumped together as "video commerce", but they are very different commitments — one is an event you staff and promote, the other is a widget you install. Here is how to tell which one your store actually needs.

Free plan · $9–$49/mo flat · Unlimited at $49 · ~5 min setup

The honest difference

Live shopping is an event. You pick a time, promote it, staff a host, stream, and sell to whoever shows up. It is genuinely powerful — urgency, real-time questions, and a host who can overcome objections live. It is also operationally heavy: you need an audience big enough that people turn up, someone comfortable on camera, and the discipline to do it repeatedly. One stream to an empty room is a wasted afternoon.

Shoppable video is infrastructure. You tag products into recorded videos and they sell around the clock, to every visitor, without anyone being present. It has no urgency and no live Q&A — but it also has no scheduling, no audience-building requirement, and no failure mode where nobody shows up.

Our take: the trifecta only closes on platforms

A note from the Quinn team.

Live commerce requires three things simultaneously: a creator worth watching, a concurrent audience watching at the same moment, and per-stream economics where one stream earns more than it cost to produce and promote. That combination closes in exactly two places — marketplaces like Amazon Live and TikTok Shop, and social platforms like TikTok and Instagram — because those platforms hand you the concurrent audience. That is the entire thing they provide.

On an independent Shopify store it does not close. You can hire or be the creator. Live-streaming technology is cheap and good, so the economics are solvable on paper. Concurrent audience is the one you cannot buy your way into — you are asking people to arrive at a set hour on a site they visit occasionally, with no algorithm pushing them there, and every stream restarts that problem from zero. Pay to fill the room and the economics collapse again. This is why independent live programmes typically stop after three or four streams: not the tech, not the host — the room stays empty.

Shoppable video is the answer to that specific problem, because it is asynchronous. Your traffic does not arrive in one 45-minute window; it arrives continuously, in ones and twos, across every time zone. Asynchronous video meets each visitor in their own moment with the same thing the stream was for — a real person showing the product, buyable on the spot — and you control which videos each surface shows them. Across thousands of Shopify stores, big and small, that is the version we have seen work.

Which should you start with?

Start with shoppable video if you have traffic but conversion is the problem, you do not have a large engaged social following yet, nobody on the team wants to host a live stream, or you simply want to know whether video moves your numbers before investing in a programme. This covers most stores.

Go to live shopping if you already have an audience that turns up when you post, a natural host (often the founder), a calendar you can actually keep, and products that benefit from real-time explanation — high-consideration, demo-heavy, or drop-driven.

The sequence most stores should follow is simple: make your existing video shoppable first, learn which content sells, and only then consider going live — because by then you know what your audience responds to, and your live replays become shoppable video too.

Questions

What is the difference between live shopping and shoppable video?

Live shopping is a scheduled live stream where viewers buy in real time; shoppable video is recorded video on your store with products tagged inside it, working around the clock. Live gives urgency and real-time Q&A but needs an audience, a host and promotion. Shoppable video needs none of those and runs continuously.

Is live shopping worth it for a small store?

Usually not, and the reason is structural. Live commerce needs a creator, a concurrent audience and per-stream economics all at once. Independent stores can solve the creator and the tech, but not concurrent audience — there is no algorithm pushing people to your storefront at 7pm, so every stream restarts audience acquisition from zero, and paying to fill the room destroys the economics. That trifecta only closes on marketplaces and social platforms.

Why is shoppable video better than live shopping for an independent brand?

Because it is asynchronous. A stream requires all your demand inside one 45-minute window, while real store traffic arrives continuously across every time zone. Shoppable video gives every visitor the same thing the stream was for — a real person showing the product, buyable immediately — at the moment they were already shopping, rather than asking them to reorganise their day around your calendar.

Can I do both live shopping and shoppable video?

Yes, and it is a good combination — live for launches and events, shoppable video for everyday conversion. Live replays can be clipped and turned into shoppable videos, so the effort of a stream keeps paying off afterwards.

Does Quinn support live shopping?
