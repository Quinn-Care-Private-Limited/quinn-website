UGC video, and how to use it

UGC video is content made by real customers and creators rather than your brand — unboxings, try-ons, honest reviews, everyday use. It consistently outperforms polished brand video for one reason: it reads as true. The gap most stores never close is the last step — turning that footage into something a shopper can actually buy from.

What UGC video is

User-generated content (UGC) video is any video about your product that you did not produce. A customer filming an unboxing, a creator doing a try-on haul, someone showing their routine. It is usually shot on a phone, vertical, unpolished, and short.

A related but distinct thing is creator content — video you commission from a paid creator in a UGC style. It looks the same, but it is produced for you and licensed, rather than posted spontaneously. Both are useful; only the first is free.

Why it outperforms brand video

  • It is believed. Shoppers discount advertising and trust peers. A customer saying a product is good carries weight a brand claim cannot.
  • It shows reality. Real lighting, real bodies, real rooms — which is exactly the information a shopper needs and a studio shoot hides.
  • It matches the feed. It looks like the content people already choose to watch, so it does not get skipped as an ad.
  • It is cheap and renewable. Your customers make more of it every week, at no cost, as long as you ask.

How to source it

  1. Harvest what exists. Check tagged posts, mentions, and your review platform for video reviews. Most stores are sitting on unused footage.
  2. Ask, systematically. Put a request in the post-purchase email and packaging insert. A small incentive — store credit, a discount, a feature on your page — lifts response sharply.
  3. Run a hashtag or challenge. Works when you already have an engaged audience.
  4. Commission creators. When you need specific products or messages covered, pay creators for UGC-style video with usage rights included.
  5. Always get permission. Ask before reusing anyone's video on your store or in ads, and keep a written record. A comment reply granting permission is the minimum; explicit rights are better, and are non-negotiable for paid media.

The step most stores miss

Collecting UGC is the easy half. The half that produces revenue is making it shoppable — tagging the products inside each clip so a shopper who is convinced can buy in one tap instead of going to search for the item. An untagged UGC gallery is decoration; a tagged one is a sales channel, and it tells you which creator's video actually earned money.

How to measure it

Track UGC the way you track any other channel: views, tap-through, add-to-carts and orders per video. That tells you which creators and which formats to invest in, and which to quietly retire. Without per-video attribution you are guessing, and guessing is what makes UGC feel unprovable.
