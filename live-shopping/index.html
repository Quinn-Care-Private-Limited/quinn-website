Live shopping, honestly assessed

Live shopping is retail's oldest format — a person showing you a product and answering questions — rebuilt for phones. It works spectacularly in some markets and quietly fails for most Western stores that try it, and the difference is almost never the platform. Here is what it actually is, what running one costs, and an honest test of whether you should bother.

What live shopping is

Live shopping (or live commerce) is a scheduled live video stream where viewers can buy the products being shown, in real time, without leaving the stream. A host demonstrates products, answers questions in the chat, and usually runs time-limited offers. Purchases happen inside the stream or through a synced product tray.

It is home shopping television with a comment box — and the comment box is the entire innovation. Being able to ask "does it come in navy, and will it fit a 34-inch chest" and get an answer in ten seconds removes the doubt that kills online conversion.

Why it is enormous in Asia and not in the West

Live commerce is a very large share of ecommerce in China and growing fast across Southeast Asia, while remaining niche in the US and Europe. The reason is not technology — it is that the surrounding infrastructure arrived in a different order. In China, live selling grew up inside the platforms people already shopped on, with an established host economy and payment flows built for it. In the West, live shopping usually asks a shopper to show up at a scheduled time on a channel they do not normally buy from, which is a much bigger request.

The practical implication: the format is proven, but the audience behaviour is not automatically portable. Copying the tactic without the audience is where most Western live programmes fail.

What it actually costs

The software is the cheap part. The real costs are:

  • A host. Someone who can talk to a camera for 45 minutes without dying. Founders are usually the best and most expensive option.
  • Promotion. A stream nobody knows about is a stream nobody watches. Budget as much effort promoting it as producing it.
  • Preparation. Product selection, running order, offers, stock checks, and a rehearsal.
  • Repetition. First streams almost always underperform. The format compounds — which means one stream tells you nothing, and a commitment to ten is the real ask.

An honest readiness test

Live shopping is likely to work for you if you can answer yes to most of these:

  1. Do you have an engaged audience that reliably reacts when you post?
  2. Do you have a natural host who is comfortable improvising on camera?
  3. Can you commit to a repeating schedule for at least a couple of months?
  4. Do your products benefit from explanation — demo-heavy, high-consideration, or fit-sensitive?
  5. Do you have drops or limited stock that create genuine urgency?

Mostly no? Live shopping is not your next move — and doing it badly costs real time for no return.

The live commerce trifecta — and why it collapses on your own store

A note from the Quinn team, based on what we see across the stores we work with.

Live commerce needs three things, and it needs all three simultaneously:

  1. A creator. Someone worth watching, who can hold attention and sell without reading a script.
  2. A concurrent audience. Enough people watching at the same moment for the format to function at all. A stream is a room. An empty room produces nothing, no matter how good the host is.
  3. Stream economics. Revenue from a single stream has to exceed what it cost to produce and promote — per stream, not eventually.

That trifecta closes in exactly two environments: marketplaces (Amazon Live, TikTok Shop) and social platforms (TikTok, Instagram). The reason is structural, not cultural — those platforms supply ingredient two. Their algorithms deliver a concurrent audience you never had to assemble. That is the entire product they are giving you.

On your own store, the trifecta does not close

As an independent Shopify brand you can solve the creator: hire one, or be one. You can solve the economics: live-streaming technology is now cheap, good, and widely available. What you cannot solve is concurrent audience.

You are asking people to show up at a specific hour, on a site they visit occasionally, with no algorithm pushing them there. Every stream starts by re-acquiring its audience from nothing. And the moment you start paying to fill the room, the economics you had just solved collapse again — you are buying traffic to attend an event instead of buying traffic that converts.

This is why independent live programmes usually die after three or four streams. It is almost never the technology and almost never the host. The room stays empty.

The asymmetry nobody mentions

A live stream requires all of your demand to arrive inside one 45-minute window. Real store traffic does precisely the opposite: it arrives continuously, in ones and twos, across every time zone, on each visitor's own schedule.

Live shopping asks your customers to reorganise their day around your calendar. That is a large request from a brand they may have discovered yesterday.

What we recommend instead, and why

Asynchronous video inverts the problem. Instead of gathering everyone into one moment, it meets every visitor in their moment — the moment they were already shopping.

This is not a watered-down substitute for live. Every visitor gets the thing the live stream existed to provide: a real person showing the product, answering the question in their head, with the item buyable right there on the screen. It simply happens on their timeline rather than yours — at 3am on a Tuesday as readily as 7pm on a Thursday — and you decide which videos each shopper sees on each surface.

Across thousands of Shopify stores, big and small, this is the version we have seen work consistently. Live commerce is a marketplace and social-platform format. On your own storefront, shoppable video is the format that fits how your traffic actually behaves.

Questions

What is live shopping?

Live shopping, or live commerce, is a scheduled live video stream where viewers can buy the products being shown in real time without leaving the stream. A host demonstrates products and answers questions in the chat, which removes the doubt that normally blocks an online purchase.

Does live shopping work for small stores?

Generally no, and the reason is structural. Live commerce needs three things at once — a creator, a concurrent audience, and per-stream economics. Independent stores can solve the creator and the economics, but not concurrent audience: you are asking people to appear at a set hour on a site they visit occasionally, with no algorithm pushing them there. That is why most independent live programmes stop after three or four streams.

Why does live shopping only work on TikTok, Instagram and marketplaces?

Because those platforms supply the hardest ingredient — a concurrent audience. Their algorithms put your stream in front of people who are already there, so you never have to assemble the room yourself. On your own storefront there is no algorithm doing that, so every stream begins by re-acquiring its audience from nothing, and paying to fill the room destroys the per-stream economics.

What should a Shopify store do instead of live shopping?

Use asynchronous shoppable video. A live stream requires all your demand to arrive inside one 45-minute window, whereas real store traffic arrives continuously, in ones and twos, across every time zone. Shoppable video gives every visitor what the stream was for — a real person showing the product, buyable on the spot — on their timeline rather than yours. Across thousands of Shopify stores, that is the version Quinn has seen work consistently.

Why is live shopping bigger in China than in the US?

It grew up inside the platforms Chinese consumers already shopped on, with an established host economy and payment flows built around it. In the West, live shopping typically asks shoppers to show up at a scheduled time on a channel they do not normally buy from, which is a much larger request.

What is the best platform for live shopping?

TikTok Live has the best discovery because the algorithm can reach non-followers; Instagram and Facebook Live reach your existing audience; Amazon Live reaches people already shopping. Streaming on your own store keeps the customer and the data but requires you to bring the whole audience yourself.

What is the difference between live shopping and shoppable video?

Live shopping is a scheduled event needing a host, an audience and promotion; shoppable video is recorded video with products tagged inside it, working continuously for every visitor. Most of the benefit comes from video of a real person showing the product, which recorded video also delivers.
