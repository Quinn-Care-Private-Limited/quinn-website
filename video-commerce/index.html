Video commerce, practically

Video commerce is the practice of selling directly through video rather than using video to advertise and hoping people find the product later. It covers shoppable video, live shopping, UGC galleries and in-video checkout — and the useful question is not whether to do it, but which format solves the problem your store actually has.

What counts as video commerce

  • Shoppable video — recorded video with products tagged inside it. Always on, low effort, the default starting point.
  • Live shopping — a scheduled live stream with real-time buying. High impact, high operational cost, needs an audience.
  • Shoppable UGC — customer and creator video, tagged. Cheapest to produce, strongest on trust.
  • Video ads that link to shoppable landing pages — paid traffic landing on video rather than a static page.

Why it works

Online retail's structural weakness is that customers cannot handle the product. Photos and copy only partly close that gap; video closes far more of it by showing motion, scale, texture and use. Video commerce adds the second half — letting the customer act on conviction the moment they feel it, instead of navigating away to find the item.

How to measure it

The mistake is measuring video with video metrics. Views and watch time tell you nothing about revenue. Insist on per-video attribution: taps, add-to-carts and orders traced to individual videos and placements. That converts video from a branding line item into a channel you can optimise — you keep what sells and cut what does not.

What it costs to start

Far less than most teams assume, because the content usually already exists — Instagram reels, TikToks, customer clips, photoshoot offcuts. Software in this category starts free and runs $9–$49/mo for most stores; the expensive mistake is metered pricing that scales up exactly when the video works. Quinn is flat, unlimited on views at $49.

Starting sensibly

  1. Put video on your top ten product pages first — highest intent, fastest signal.
  2. Add a UGC wall or stories bar to the homepage for trust.
  3. Make your Instagram and TikTok content shoppable so social traffic converts on-site.
  4. Check attribution monthly, keep winners, replace losers.
  5. Only consider live once you have an audience that reliably shows up.

Questions

What is video commerce?

Video commerce is selling directly through video — shoppable video, live shopping, shoppable UGC and in-video checkout — rather than using video purely for awareness. The defining feature is that a viewer can buy from inside the video instead of navigating away to find the product.

What is the difference between video commerce and video marketing?

Video marketing aims to build awareness and demand; video commerce closes the transaction inside the video. The same clip can serve both, but video commerce requires product tagging and a buying path, plus attribution linking videos to orders.

How do I measure video commerce?

Not with views or watch time. Measure taps, add-to-carts and orders attributed to individual videos and placements, so you can tell which content produced revenue and drop what did not.

How do I start with video commerce?

Start with shoppable video on your top ten product pages, using content you already have — reels, TikToks, customer clips. Add a UGC wall for trust, make your social feeds shoppable, then review per-video attribution monthly.
