Adding a TikTok feed to your website

TikTok is where the discovery happens and your site is where the buying happens, so the bridge between them matters. Embedding TikTok videos on your own site keeps that momentum — provided the embed does not wreck your page speed and the videos do not dead-end at TikTok.

The options

TikTok's official embed

Every TikTok has an embed code: Share → Embed → copy, then paste into a page. Free and official, but one video per embed, updated by hand, and it loads TikTok's player script — which is not light.

An app or widget

Pulls in videos automatically and renders them in a layout you control, usually with better performance handling than the raw embed. On Shopify this is the practical route.

Download and self-host

Download your own videos and serve them yourself. You get full control and no third-party script, but you now own hosting, compression and delivery — which is where most self-hosted video goes wrong. Only publish videos you own the rights to.

Keep the page fast

TikTok embeds are heavy: player script, thumbnails and video. Lazy-load anything below the fold, limit how many you place on one page, never autoplay with sound, and prefer a tool that compresses and serves video itself over one that iframes the platform player. Quinn compresses up to 20× with an in-house AI encoder and lazy-loads from a CDN.

The bigger miss: TikToks that cannot be bought

A TikTok on your product page is doing the persuading. If tapping it opens TikTok, you have spent your best asset sending someone off your store. Making it shoppable — product tagged, add-to-cart in place — keeps the buyer where they can buy, and reports which video produced the order.

Questions

How do I embed a TikTok video on my website?

Open the video, choose Share then Embed, copy the code, and paste it into your page's HTML. That handles one video at a time and loads TikTok's player script; for a maintained feed of several videos, an app is easier and usually faster.

Do TikTok embeds slow down a website?

Yes, they can — the official embed loads a third-party player script plus thumbnails and video. Lazy-load below-the-fold embeds, limit how many appear on a page, and consider a tool that compresses and serves the video itself rather than iframing TikTok's player.

Can I make TikTok videos shoppable on my store?

Yes. Import them into a shoppable video app and tag the products that appear in each. On Shopify, Quinn imports TikToks by link and turns them into tappable product cards with add-to-cart, plus per-video order attribution.

Can I use any TikTok video on my website?

Only ones you have the rights to — your own videos, or a creator's with explicit permission. Reposting someone else's content without permission is a rights problem regardless of how you embed it.
