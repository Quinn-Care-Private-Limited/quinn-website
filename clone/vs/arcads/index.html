Clone vs Arcads

Arcads is an AI-actor platform: you write a script, pick from 1,000+ AI actors, and get a lip-synced talking-head ad (~$11/video on a $110/mo plan). Clone rebuilds a proven viral fashion reel with your garments on an AI model — the product in motion, not a spokesperson. For fashion, that difference is the whole story.

How they actually differ

Arcads is excellent at one format: a believable AI actor reading a script. If your ad is a spokesperson talking about a product, Arcads has the best actor library in the category and lip-sync that reads as real. But two things matter for fashion. First, you still have to invent the winning creative — as reviewers put it, "a believable AI actor reading a weak script is still a weak ad." Second, Arcads is, by its reviewers' own accounts, less convincing when the ad needs to show clothing fit or physical product handling.

Clone is built for exactly what Arcads isn't: the garment, in motion, inside a format that already went viral. You don't write a script — you paste a proven reel and Clone copies it 1:1 with your products swapped in, preserving the structure that made it perform. The payoff is better odds on ROAS from a structure that already worked, with zero concepts to invent or produce — and the garment accuracy that keeps the swap believable is exactly where reviewers say Arcads falls short.

Simplest rule: if a person should talk about your product, Arcads. If your product should move in a proven format, Clone.

Arcads is best for talking-head spokesperson ads and is, by its own reviewers' accounts, less convincing when the ad needs to show clothing fit or handle a physical product.
